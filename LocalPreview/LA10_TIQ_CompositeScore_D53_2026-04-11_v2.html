Three Known Failure Modes
N6
Zone Masking via N6 Dominance
Checking...
When N6 (endocrine signalling) burden is very high, it dominates the composite score across all four zones it participates in (Z1, Z2, Z3, Z5). This can mask lower burden in the remaining nodes and inflate the apparent zone composite. N6 dampening at 0.7x mitigates this, but does not eliminate it. Flag: if N6 raw score exceeds 80 and zone composite is above 70, review individual node scores before accepting TIQ at face value.
TH
Threshold Artefact
Checking...
A zone scoring at 41 (just above the 40-point Z1-Z4 threshold) contributes as an active zone to TIQ. A zone scoring at 39 (just below) does not. A 2-point difference in input produces a discrete jump in TIQ output. This is not a bug: it reflects the clinical reality of threshold activation. Practitioners should treat near-threshold zones (35-39 for Z1-Z4, 27-31 for Z5) as monitoring zones and not assume they are definitively inactive.
N6
N6 Cascade Across All Zones
Checking...
N6 (endocrine signalling) participates in Z1, Z2, Z3, and Z5. If N6 burden is extreme (internal score above 85), TIQ will show very high composite burden even when individual system burdens are moderate. This is architecturally correct: N6 dysregulation does genuinely affect four zones simultaneously. However, it means TIQ composite can reach high values from a single node failure rather than a true multi-zone clinical picture. DRD™ designation should be reviewed carefully in N6-dominant cases.

TIQ™ Architecture Notes
TIQ is a composite index: it summarises terrain complexity in a single number for practitioner orientation and patient communication. It is not a primary clinical decision input. DRD™ designation and individual zone scores govern protocol design. TIQ governs the pace and intensity of the approach. A TIQ of 2-3 warrants a slower, deeper protocol horizon than a TIQ of 7-8.
